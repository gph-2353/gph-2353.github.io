Today: one analysis, start to finish

How is ground-level ozone related to weather in New York City?

  • Ozone is a respiratory irritant: asthma attacks, ER visits, excess deaths on high-ozone days
  • Data: airquality, built into R. Daily measurements in NYC, May to September 1973: Ozone (ppb, Roosevelt Island), Solar.R (solar radiation, langleys), Wind (mph, LaGuardia), Temp (degrees F)

Your call: the missing days

37 of 153 ozone values are missing. We could:

  • A. Analyze complete cases only (111 days) and say so
  • B. Fill in missing ozone with the mean ozone value
  • C. Ignore the issue and let lm() silently do A for us
aq <- airquality |> drop_na(Ozone, Solar.R, Wind, Temp)
nrow(aq)
[1] 111

A and C fit the same model; A is honest about it. B is worse: it invents 37 identical fake days and shrinks every SE. We choose A and admit the risk: if monitors fail more on extreme days, complete cases are biased.

Step 4: diagnostics on the raw-scale model

  • Residuals vs fitted: a clear U-shape (curvature) and a funnel
  • Q-Q: a heavy right tail

Step 4: the diagnostics in numbers

  • Funnel: residual sd nearly doubles from the middle to the top third of fitted values
  • Right tail: the model misses high-ozone days by up to 95 ppb
  • Formal checks agree: Tukey curvature test p < 0.001, non-constant variance test p = 0.02, Shapiro-Wilk p < 0.001

Your call: the remedy

Curvature, a funnel, and right skew, all at once. We could:

  • A. Log-transform the outcome: log(Ozone)
  • B. Add a squared term, Temp^2
  • C. Drop the high-ozone days as outliers

C destroys data: high-ozone days are the public health point. B chases the curvature but not the skew or funnel. A is the classic move when all three appear together; week 8’s Box-Cox agrees (lambda about 0.2, close to log), and log keeps interpretability.

Step 6: did the remedy work?

  • Curvature: essentially gone
  • Right tail: fixed (Shapiro-Wilk p = 0.06)

Step 6: an honest wrinkle

  • The low end is now the noisy part: one day with Ozone = 1 ppb has residual -2.1
  • A transformation moves problems around; it does not owe us perfection
  • Verdict: clearly better, not flawless. We proceed, saying so

Step 7: start bigger, then prune

  • airquality also records Month and Day. Could the season matter beyond temperature? Could ozone drift within a month?

  • Start from a larger model, adding factor(Month) and Day, and let backward elimination (Week 10) prune it

  • Every candidate is fit to the same 111 days and the same response, log(Ozone), so AIC and BIC comparisons are fair

Your call: which model?

  • A. Keep our three predictors; no comparison needed
  • B. Keep the big model: extra variables cannot hurt
  • C. Run backward elimination with step() using AIC, and check whether BIC agrees

C. A skips a step your report is graded on (model comparison). B pays a parameter for every month and keeps variables that add noise, not information.

Step 7: backward elimination

full <- lm(log(Ozone) ~ Solar.R + Wind + Temp + factor(Month) + Day,
           data = aq)
sel_aic <- step(full, direction = "backward", trace = 0)
sel_bic <- step(full, direction = "backward", k = log(nrow(aq)), trace = 0)
formula(sel_aic)
log(Ozone) ~ Solar.R + Wind + Temp
formula(sel_bic)
log(Ozone) ~ Solar.R + Wind + Temp

Step 7: what selection did and did not do

  • It checked our model against a larger one; it did not discover a new one
  • Report the path in Methods: “Starting from a model that also included month and day of month, backward elimination by AIC removed both; BIC agreed.”
  • Selection used these same 111 days, so the final p-values are a little optimistic (Week 10B)

Step 9: the final model, strong-form

Because the outcome is logged, coefficients become percent changes (e^{\hat\beta} - 1):

  • Temp: each additional degree F is associated with about 5% higher ozone (95% CI 4% to 6%), holding wind and solar radiation fixed
  • Wind: each additional mph of wind is associated with about 6% lower ozone, holding the others fixed (dilution and transport)

Step 9: solar radiation and scope

  • Solar.R: each additional langley is associated with about 0.25% higher ozone
  • Model describes daily NYC ozone, summer 1973; R^2 = 0.66 on the log scale

Step 10: honest limitations of the data

  • 1973, one city, one monitor. Emissions, traffic, and regulation have changed profoundly; nothing here transfers to 2026 without new data
  • Observational. Weather is not randomized; hot days differ from cool days in many ways (traffic, energy use). These are associations
  • Complete-case analysis of 111 of 153 days; if missingness tracks ozone levels, estimates are biased

Step 10: honest limitations of the model

  • No time structure modeled. Consecutive days are correlated; standard errors are likely somewhat optimistic
  • Selected on the same data. step() used these 111 days, so p-values and CIs are a little optimistic too
