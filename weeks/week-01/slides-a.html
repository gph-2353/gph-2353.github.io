What is a regression question?

All of these are regression questions:

  • How much does systolic blood pressure rise per decade of age, and does the rate differ between smokers and non-smokers?
  • Which county characteristics best predict premature death rates, and how well?
  • After adjusting for age and income, is sleep duration associated with BMI?

Common structure: a continuous outcome y, one or more predictors x, and a question about the relationship.

Three jobs a regression model can do

  1. Description: summarize how y varies with x in these data
  2. Prediction: given a new x, what y should we expect, and how sure are we?
  3. Explanation: what happens to y if x changes? (causal, the hardest)

Warning

Same lm() output, three very different claims. Most regression disasters come from doing job 1 and announcing job 3.

Where it all started: Galton’s heights

Francis Galton (1886) collected heights of parents and their adult children.

Code 
data("GaltonFamilies")
ggplot(GaltonFamilies, aes(midparentHeight, childHeight)) +
  geom_point(alpha = 0.3) +
  geom_smooth(method = "lm", se = FALSE) +
  geom_abline(slope = 1, intercept = 0, linetype = 2, color = "red") +
  labs(x = "Mid-parent height (in)", y = "Adult child height (in)",
       title = "Galton's families: the original regression")

Regression to the mean

  • The fitted line (blue) is flatter than the identity line (red, slope 1)
  • Tall parents have tall children, but less tall than themselves on average; short parents likewise
  • Galton called it “regression towards mediocrity”; the name stuck to the whole method

Think-pair-share (2 min): A clinic measures blood pressure twice. Patients flagged as hypertensive at visit 1 tend to look better at visit 2 even with no treatment. Why? What would this do to a naive before-after study of a new drug?

Regression to the mean is everywhere

  • The “sophomore slump” and magazine-cover jinx
  • Speed cameras placed at intersections after crash spikes “work”
  • Extreme baseline patients improve in single-arm trials

This is why we need control groups and why understanding regression deeply matters for public health practice, not just for fitting lines.

NHANES caveats (say them once, remember all semester)

  • Our version is a teaching extract: resampled, survey weights ignored
  • Cross-sectional: one time point per person
  • Self-reported variables (sleep, alcohol) measured with error

Fine for learning regression; for a real paper you would use the raw NHANES files with survey methods.
