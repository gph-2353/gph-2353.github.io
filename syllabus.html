Course Description

Regression models are one of the most important statistical techniques used in public health. This course focuses on data analysis that uses linear regression models for continuous outcomes. The first part of this course introduces simple and multiple linear regressions, principles of ordinary least square regression models, model assumptions, and inferences about model parameters. The second part of the course focuses on important practical matters, such as prediction, diagnostics, and variable selection. These two parts together provide the foundations for more advanced statistical modeling. Examples are drawn from broad areas of public health research. All the analyses will be taught and performed using R statistical software.

Course Overview

This course emphasizes modern, reproducible, and active statistical practice in public health. Class sessions follow a hands-on studio rhythm: short conceptual mini-lectures alternate with guided data analyses and paired coding labs in RStudio using real-world health datasets (including NHANES, US county health metrics, and clinical trials). All computational work is done using R and Quarto. In addition, an integrated “AI Policy & Practice” component trains students to use modern AI assistants productively and defensively for exploratory coding while developing the rigorous statistical judgment required to verify, interpret, and critique quantitative outputs.

Policies

Technology & Generative AI Policy (Three Zones)

  1. Zone 1: Permitted with Disclosure & Critique (Homework & Final Project) — You are encouraged to use AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot) for exploratory coding, debugging, and ideation. When used, you must document your prompt strategy in an AI Appendix and take 100% intellectual responsibility for verifying all calculations.
  2. Zone 2: Strictly Prohibited (Warm-Up Quizzes & Final Exam) — No electronic devices, internet access, or AI assistants are permitted during quizzes and exams.
  3. Zone 3: Human-First (In-Class Labs) — Work with your human lab partner first before consulting AI assistants.

For full details, see the dedicated AI Policy page.
