Course Project: Public Health Regression Study

In groups of up to two, you will carry out an end-to-end regression analysis of a real-world public health dataset of your choice: pose an impactful health question, explore and clean the data, formulate and refine multiple regression models, run diagnostic checks, and communicate your findings in both an oral presentation and a written manuscript.

The project is worth 30% of the course grade, spread across four milestones so that feedback arrives while it can still shape your analysis.

Milestone 1: Proposal (1 page, 3%)

State your research question, name your public health data source (with a direct link and proof of loadability), identify your continuous outcome and at least 4 candidate predictors (with units), and note any initial data challenges (missingness, sample size, skewness).

Rubric (3 pts): - Feasible dataset in hand (1 pt) - Clear continuous-outcome question suitable for linear regression (1 pt) - Sensible candidate predictors with units described (1 pt)

Milestone 2: EDA Memo (2–3 pages, 5%)

Provide univariate distributions of outcome and key predictors, at least three informative bivariate plots (with smooths/faceting), an honest accounting of missing data, and a summary of expected modeling relationships and anticipated challenges (non-linearity, collinearity, outliers).

Rubric (5 pts): - Outcome described with plots and summary statistics (1 pt) - Predictor-outcome relationships explored graphically (2 pts) - Missing data and data quality issues addressed honestly (1 pt) - Modeling plan updated in light of exploratory findings (1 pt)

Milestone 3: Presentation (10 min + 3 min Q&A, 7%)

Both partners must speak. Your presentation should cover: 1. Research question and public health motivation (1 slide). 2. Data source and one key EDA finding. 3. Your primary fitted multiple regression model with strong-form coefficient interpretations. 4. One diagnostic test/plot you investigated and how you resolved or acknowledged it. 5. Key limitations and implications.

Rubric (7 pts): - Clarity of research question and public health context (1 pt) - Correctness of statistical models and estimates (2 pts) - Precision of coefficient interpretations in words (2 pts) - Diagnostic checks honestly presented (1 pt) - Delivery, teamwork, and timing (1 pt)

Milestone 4: Final Report (Max 8 pages + Appendix, 15%)

Written in Quarto and rendered to a clean PDF report formatted with standard sections: Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion. An unlimited appendix may contain raw code, full model outputs, and your AI-Use Appendix.

Rubric (15 pts): - Introduction (2 pts): Research question, public health background, data source. - Methods (3 pts): Model specifications, justification of variable selection and transformations, inference framework. - Results (6 pts): Point estimates with strong-form interpretations (2 pts), statistical inference & hypothesis tests (1 pt), thorough diagnostic checks (2 pts), model comparisons (1 pt). - Discussion (2 pts): Public health implications, limitations, confounding, and causal cautions. - Reproducibility & AI Appendix (2 pts): Fully reproducible Quarto workflow; transparent disclosure of AI assistance.

Recommended Public Health Datasets

Any publicly available dataset with a continuous outcome and sufficient sample size (n \ge 200) is eligible:

  1. County Health Rankings: County-level health outcomes, clinical care, and socioeconomic factors (countyhealthrankings.org)
  2. CDC NHANES: Continuous national survey on nutrition and metabolic health (wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes)
  3. CDC BRFSS: Behavioral risk factors and chronic disease surveillance (cdc.gov/brfss)
  4. CDC PLACES: Census-tract and local health outcome estimates (cdc.gov/places)
  5. WHO Global Health Observatory: Cross-country health and life-expectancy metrics (who.int/data/gho)
  6. EPA Outdoor Air Quality Data: Environmental exposures and particulate matter (epa.gov/outdoor-air-quality-data)
  7. NYC Open Data: Citywide community health surveys, air quality, and vital statistics (opendata.cityofnewyork.us)
  8. IPUMS Health Surveys: Harmonized NHIS and MEPS microdata (healthsurveys.ipums.org)
  9. Global Burden of Disease (GBD): Disability-adjusted life years and disease burden (ghdx.healthdata.org)
  10. TidyTuesday Health Archives: Curated public health and epidemiological datasets (github.com/rfordatascience/tidytuesday)
