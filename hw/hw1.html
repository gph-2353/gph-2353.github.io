Data. The Baystate Medical Center birth weight study (Springfield, MA, 1986), 189 births:

library(tidyverse)
data(birthwt, package = "MASS")
births <- birthwt |>
  mutate(smoke = factor(smoke, labels = c("Non-smoker", "Smoker")))

Variables used below: bwt = birth weight (grams), lwt = mother’s pre-pregnancy weight (pounds), age = mother’s age (years), smoke = smoking during pregnancy.

Section 1: Do (6 points)

1.1 Describe the outcome bwt: a histogram plus a short numerical summary. Two sentences on center, spread, and shape, with units. (2 pts)

1.2 Fit the simple linear regression of bwt on lwt. Report the fitted equation and add the fitted line to a scatterplot (use your coefficients with geom_abline, not geom_smooth). (2 pts)

1.3 Using arithmetic on your coefficients, give the model’s expected birth weight for mothers weighing 120 and 200 pounds. State which prediction you trust more, and why, referring to the data. (2 pts)

Section 2: Interpret (8 points; interpretation precision counts double)

2.1 Interpret the slope from 1.2 in one strong-form sentence. (2 pts)

2.2 The R-squared of this model is about 0.03. A journalist concludes the model is “useless.” Give a two-sentence rebuttal that concedes what the model cannot do and states what the slope estimate still offers. (2 pts)

2.3 Fit bwt ~ smoke, then bwt ~ smoke + lwt. Report the smoking coefficient from both. Does adjusting for mother’s weight change the smoking association much here? Using the group means of lwt by smoking status, explain in one sentence why the adjustment behaves the way it does. (2 pts)

2.4 Write the strong-form interpretation of the smoking coefficient in the adjusted model, then a second sentence stating clearly one reason this is not an estimate of the causal effect of smoking cessation. (2 pts)

Section 3: Critique the AI (6 points)

A student pasted this dataset description into an AI assistant and asked for an analysis. The assistant replied:

“I fit a regression of birth weight on mother’s age and found a slope of 12.4 grams per year. Since the intercept is 2656 grams, a newborn whose mother is age zero would weigh 2656 grams. The p-value for age was 0.19, which proves that mother’s age has no effect on birth weight. Because heavier mothers tend to be older, I also recommend removing lwt from any model that includes age, since two correlated predictors cannot be used together in linear regression. Overall, since this regression is based on a proper hospital sample, the slope of 12.4 tells us how much a mother’s baby will gain if she delays pregnancy by one year.”

3.1 This paragraph contains at least four distinct statistical errors. Identify each one you find (quote the offending phrase), and explain in one or two sentences why it is wrong. Finding four earns full credit; a fifth earns a bonus point. (4 pts + 1 bonus)

3.2 Verify one of the AI’s numerical claims by fitting bwt ~ age yourself. Report whether the slope and intercept match, and write the correct one-sentence conclusion about the age association that the data actually support. (2 pts)
